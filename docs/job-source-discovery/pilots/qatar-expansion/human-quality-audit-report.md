## 3. Explicitly investigated relationships

- **QatarEnergy vs. QatarEnergy LNG**: Correctly resolved as `duplicate_or_alias` of `cc-qatarenergy` in the prior pass — QatarEnergy LNG's careers portal is already referenced in the existing verified `cc-qatarenergy` row's notes. **No change needed; audit confirms this decision was correct.**
- **Minor Hotels vs. individual brands (Anantara, Tivoli)**: The supplied lead "Minor Hotels" itself was correctly resolved as `duplicate_or_alias` (parent umbrella already covered by the two staged brand rows). However, auditing against the **existing** registry surfaced a real inconsistency: `master-company-registry.csv` already contains group-level entries for **Accor** (`cc-accor`, UAE, one row covering the whole portfolio including the Ibis brand by name) and **Hilton** (`cc-hilton-uae`, one row covering multiple Dubai/Abu Dhabi properties). This Qatar pass instead modeled Accor (Pullman, ibis) and Hilton (Salwa) at the **individual-property** level — a finer granularity than the established precedent for the *same global brands*. Minor Hotels itself was never previously in the registry, so Anantara/Tivoli don't directly conflict with an existing precedent, but the same open policy question applies to them for consistency. **This is the single most significant structural finding of this audit** — see Section 5 for the required product-owner decision.
- **Anantara, Tivoli, Pullman, Hilton Salwa, Rosewood Doha, ibis**: Individually verified as real, distinct properties (see Table 1). Rosewood Doha's location was correctly self-corrected to Lusail in the original pass. Hilton Salwa's exact municipality was **not** independently confirmed (flagged C29).
- **Qatar Airways and affiliated service brands**: Qatar Airways itself is already-present (unchanged). No staged row in this batch represents a Qatar Airways-affiliated brand (e.g. Qatar Duty Free, Qatar Aviation Services) — no conflict found.
- **QNB Group and other bank/group identities**: QNB (Qatar National Bank) and Commercial Bank of Qatar are both already-present and were correctly excluded from re-staging. No new row in this batch collides with either.
- **Starlink Qatar vs. unrelated global Starlink entities**: Correctly kept **out** of the staged 38 and routed to `manual-review-queue.csv` in the original pass, specifically because of this exact conflation risk with SpaceX's Starlink. Audit confirms this was the right call — if ever promoted, `company_name`/`legal_or_official_name` must explicitly retain "Qatar" and the Ooredoo Group affiliation to prevent conflation.
- **Commercial Bank naming**: Already-present, correctly deduplicated, no new row created.
- **International Schools Partnership vs. individual Qatar schools**: ISP is modeled as **one** row covering both of its Qatar schools (Park House English School, The Hamilton International School), justified by ISP running a single shared ATS with a country-level filter — unlike the hotel brands, where hiring happens on **per-property** boards. This distinction is defensible (different underlying ATS structure), but is worth a deliberate product-owner confirmation given the hotel-brand precedent went the other way.
- **Scale AI's actual Qatar presence**: Investigated specifically per the task's request. Evidence (an Executive Assistant role explicitly supporting "three executives in their day to day roles" in Doha, plus a "Strategist, Qatar" role growing the Global Public Sector business unit) is reasonably convincing of genuine physical presence, not merely remote/traveling consulting — kept as Qatar-presence confirmed, but the `industry` tag was found to be wrong (see C28).
- **Remote employers with unclear Qatar offices**: None of the 38 staged rows are remote-labeled — all have a specific on-site Qatar `target_city`. No field-vs-site-work mislabeling was found within this batch.
- **Recruitment/staffing companies that might also hire internally**: Explicitly re-examined for all 6 sources. Vistas Global shows the clearest internal-hire signal (Recruitment Officer/Sourcing Officer roles read as genuinely internal), with Edison Smart and Kingston Stanley showing weaker but plausible signals. None were promoted to employer status this pass — all three are flagged for a dedicated employer-angle follow-up.

## 6. Remaining schema / product-owner decisions

- **Hotel-brand granularity policy**: Should Accor and Hilton properties in Qatar be modeled per-property (as this pass did) or per-group (matching the existing `cc-accor`/`cc-hilton-uae` UAE precedent)? This decision affects C27, C29, and C35 directly, and retroactively raises the question of whether the UAE rows should eventually be split to match a per-property standard instead. This is a genuine policy choice, not a data-quality defect, and blocks final canonical-ID assignment for those three rows.
- **School-group granularity policy**: Confirm whether ISP's single-row-with-country-filter model (C06) is the intended long-term pattern, or whether individual Qatar schools should eventually get their own rows to match the hotel-property convention.
- **Industry field strictness**: The wider `master-company-registry.csv` shows `industry` is free text in practice across markets (highly inconsistent casing/format), while `qatar.csv` itself has so far stayed to a clean snake_case subset. Several new values introduced this pass (`defense_aerospace`, `conglomerate`, `industrial_services`, `ai_data_training` — the last already used elsewhere in `international-remote.csv`) are reasonable but were not previously used within `qatar.csv` specifically. Recommend a product-owner decision on whether `qatar.csv` should keep a stricter internal convention than the rest of the registry.
- **Edison Smart, Kingston Stanley, Vistas Global employer-angle follow-up**: A dedicated future pass should determine whether any of these three deserve their own `qatar.csv` employer row for confirmed-internal (non-client-placement) hiring.
